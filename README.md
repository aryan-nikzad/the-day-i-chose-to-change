### The recovery protocol I discovered

1. **Take a shower.** Reset physically and mentally.
2. **Complete the remaining prayers.** The evening and night prayers are Maghrib and Isha. (If I meant a different pair, I can correct this wording later.)
3. **Plan tomorrow's task list in Fuzzy Journey**, my scheduling and work-planning app.
4. **Go to sleep as soon as reasonably possible.** Do not prolong the night with more analysis or distractions.
5. **Wake at the time scheduled by Fuzzy Journey.** Treat that wake-up time as the anchor for restarting the routine. Do not deliberately stay awake all night, use excessive caffeine, or put safety at risk to force it; if sleep loss makes driving or other hazardous activity unsafe, choose safety and resume the plan as soon as possible.
6. **Start following Fuzzy Journey's schedule.** The first step should include making wudu (وضو) and performing the Dhuhr/noon prayer at its proper time. Do not neglect prayer; follow the actual prayer time rather than treating a scheduled clock time as permission to pray outside it.
7. **Do not keep planning the start. Start working.** Once the next task is clear, begin it. No more postponing action in order to perfect the plan.

This is the recovery sequence I found most effective. It turns a disrupted day into a concrete reset: shower, prayer, prepare tomorrow, sleep, wake, return to the schedule, and work. **The purpose of planning is to get me moving—not to become another way to delay starting.**

## November 19, 2026 — The Missing Exception: Disruption Days

I have discovered a serious flaw in my planning system: it assumes that I will always have the physical and mental capacity to follow my normal schedule.

That assumption is unrealistic.

Three days ago, I became sick. A cold or another illness can reduce my energy, concentration, and ability to work. My normal plan did not account for this. As a result, I lost control of the schedule and ended up with days that did not go as planned.

And illness is only one example. There are circumstances that nobody can reasonably plan around in advance:

- **Illness and medical conditions:** colds, infections, injuries, pain, exhaustion, and other health problems.
- **Death and bereavement:** losing someone important, or having to support someone close to me through a loss.
- **War and serious instability:** attacks, conflict, danger, displacement, power or internet outages, and the consequences of living in an unstable environment.
- **Emergencies and major personal events:** accidents, family crises, urgent responsibilities, and other events that genuinely take priority over the schedule.

These are not ordinary distractions. They are exceptional circumstances that can change what I am capable of doing on a given day.

### The solution: Disruption Mode in Fuzzy Journey

I will add a dedicated **Disruption Mode** to Fuzzy Journey.

Instead of abandoning the schedule and doing whatever happens to happen, I will deliberately switch to a smaller, more realistic version of my day.

My initial proposal is to reduce the day's planned workload to **25% of its normal target**.

The purpose is not to force myself to work through an illness or an emergency. It is to preserve a manageable amount of structure when I am still capable of doing something, rather than letting an unexpected event turn into an entirely uncontrolled day.

The principle is:

> When life makes the normal plan impossible, I do not have to abandon planning. I change the scale of the plan to match reality.
>
> I am not required to perform at 100% capacity every day. I am responsible for responding intelligently to the capacity I actually have.

### Principles for this mode

1. **Deliberate activation:** Disruption Mode should be an intentional choice, not an automatic punishment for missing tasks.
2. **Reduced workload:** Start with a target of 25% of the normal daily workload. This is a planning target, not a mandatory minimum.
3. **Preserve control:** Keep a simple, visible structure for the day instead of letting the entire schedule become undefined.
4. **Health and safety first:** If I am too sick, exhausted, distressed, or unsafe to work, rest and essential care take priority. Zero work can be the correct choice.
5. **No accumulated debt:** Uncompleted tasks should not automatically be crammed into tomorrow. Reassess and reschedule them deliberately.
6. **Return without punishment:** When the exceptional circumstances pass, return to the normal schedule progressively and without treating the disruption as a personal failure.

The exact implementation and workload calculation will be developed in Fuzzy Journey.

**A plan that only works when everything goes perfectly is an incomplete plan. I need a system that also works when life does not.**

Disruption Mode is not an excuse to give up. It is a contingency plan for circumstances that cannot always be prevented.

The objective remains unchanged: stay in control of my decisions, adapt to reality, and continue building the life I chose—without sacrificing my health or ignoring genuine emergencies.

### November 19, 2026 — The Plan Must Live in My Mind, Not Only in Software

I need to establish a more fundamental rule about planning: **the plan must be stored and understood in my own mind. Fuzzy Journey is not the owner of my plan; I am.**

I tell the computer my plan so it can act as a safety check, schedule, and reminder. It should help me notice something if I forget it, but it must not become the only place where the plan exists.

My intended relationship is similar to the relationship between RAM and persistent storage:

- **My brain is the primary place where the plan lives.** I should understand it, remember its important rules, and be able to follow it without depending on a screen.
- **The computer is the secondary backup and reminder.** It can retain details, check the schedule, remind me of forgotten items, and help me verify that I have not overlooked something.
- **The computer must not replace my own awareness.** I should not become passive and wait for software to tell me how to live my day.

The software is there to support my memory, not to become my memory. I must remain capable of following the plan even when the computer is unavailable, switched off, or disconnected.

### Focus, order, and avoiding the distraction cycle

One of my biggest difficulties is **تمرکز (focus)**. I have noticed that my concentration becomes much worse when I leave my plan and lose the structure of the day.

My current personal explanation is that excessive pornography and masturbation, too much reliance on the computer, and repeated high-stimulation habits may be contributing to my difficulty concentrating and to symptoms that can resemble ADHD. I do not want to treat that explanation as a medical diagnosis or claim that I have proved the cause. What I can record with certainty is the pattern I have noticed: when I stop following a structured plan, I become more vulnerable to distraction and to habits I am trying to leave behind.

Order matters to me because it makes the day predictable. A clear routine gives me fewer opportunities to drift into unplanned stimulation and distraction. **Staying on the plan is therefore not merely about productivity; it is one of the safeguards I want to use to protect my attention and self-control.**

I should not respond to a lapse by declaring the whole day lost. I should return to the next planned action, without turning the mistake into a larger spiral.

### The 25% day still has structure and personal commitments

Disruption Mode reduces the workload; it does not mean that I abandon every part of my routine.

Even on a 25% day, I want to preserve my regular personal anchors and planned activities:

- Perform my prayers.
- Exercise.
- Read my book.
- Continue the other activities that are part of my normal routine.

The 25% target is about reducing the workload, not casually deleting the habits and commitments that give the day its structure. These activities should remain in the schedule, with sensible adjustments if the actual illness, injury, or emergency makes a particular activity unsafe or impossible. If I genuinely cannot carry them out, health and safety take priority rather than forcing them.

There is also a second, more complete fallback for a day when even a reduced schedule is not realistic:

**Stay home and rest, sleep, or remain awake without using the computer. The computer stays off.** This is an intentional offline recovery day, not an invitation to replace work with hours of screen-based distraction.

### The computer is for work only

I need a firm boundary around computer use:

> **The computer is a work tool. It is not my entertainment device or my social hangout.**

- Use the computer for work and work-related tasks only.
- Do not use it to chat with friends, casually browse, or seek entertainment.

This boundary is intended to reduce unnecessary stimulation and prevent the computer from pulling me away from the life and routine I am trying to build.

**The plan lives in my mind. The computer checks and reminds. My routine protects my focus. The computer serves my work—and nothing else.**
